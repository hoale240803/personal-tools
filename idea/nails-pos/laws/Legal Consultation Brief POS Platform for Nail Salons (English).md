## 1. Purpose of the consultation

I am preparing to build a point-of-sale (POS) and digital marketing software company serving nail salons in the United States. I need an attorney to help identify the main legal risks, set priorities, and define the minimum set of documents I need before onboarding real customers.

## 2. Business overview

- **Product:** POS software for nail salons covering check-in, technician turn queue, appointment calendar, checkout, tips, gift cards, loyalty points, SMS (appointment reminders, birthday offers, win-back promotions), technician income reports, and tracking of cash owed to technicians.
- **Differentiators:** (1) a revenue-share commission rate different from competitors; (2) salon-owned hardware (card terminals, printers) can be reused when a salon switches to my system; (3) fast support through a two-tier hotline (tier 1 trained in nail salon operations, tier 2 technical).
- **Vision:** after nail salons, expand to restaurants and small grocery stores using the same core platform.
- **Development approach:** AI agents plus, possibly, an external software development company (expected to be in Vietnam). I act as product owner.
- **Payments:** I do not want to become a payment company. I expect to partner with a processor or an ISO/ISV partner under a referral or integration model.
- **Stage:** very early. No paying customers yet. Entity formed: \[fill in: yes/no\]. My legal budget is limited, so I need a phased approach.
- **Additional context:** my family runs a nail salon that uses a competitor's system (MAC USA / ABC Salon System). I used screenshots of that system to understand features for planning purposes.

## 3. Questions for the attorney

### A. Entity and ownership

1. Should I form an LLC or another entity, in Michigan or Delaware, considering possible future investors or partners?
2. What documents do I need (operating agreement, EIN, separate bank account) to separate personal and company assets?
3. How should I assign source code, domain names, and branding I created before forming the entity?

### B. SMS and marketing compliance (TCPA)

1. How should I collect and store proof of consent from salon customers so reminders and promotions are lawful?
2. Who is liable if a salon sends non-compliant messages: the platform or the salon? Which contract terms shift that risk to the salon?
3. What opt-out (STOP) process, sending-hour limits, and carrier campaign registration (A2P 10DLC) are required, and how do transactional reminders differ from promotional texts?
4. What is the class-action exposure and how can I reduce it?

### C. Payments and hardware

1. Which model (referral, ISO, ISV, integration) lets me avoid money transmitter licensing and heavy PCI responsibility?
2. Which clauses in a payment partner agreement need attention: ownership of the merchant portfolio, exclusivity, termination fees, revenue share, and clawbacks?
3. Could a promise that salons can reuse their hardware be considered an overpromise if terminals are locked by the previous provider? How should it be worded?
4. Are there legal limits or special rules on charging a commission on a salon's revenue?

### D. Contracts and terms with customers (salon owners)

1. What is the minimum document set: subscription agreement or MSA, terms of service, privacy policy, acceptable use policy, and SLA?
2. Limitation of liability, indemnification, auto-renewal, early termination fees, dispute resolution (governing law, arbitration or courts).
3. Data ownership and export rights when a customer terminates.

### E. Data and privacy

1. My obligations regarding salon customers' data (phone numbers, birthdays, loyalty points).
2. Breach notification requirements under Michigan law and the laws of other states where customers are located.
3. Data retention and deletion policy, and handling deletion requests from end users.

### F. Competition and intellectual property

1. Does the salon's contract with MAC USA restrict reverse engineering, sharing screenshots, or competition? What should I do to avoid breach or claims?
2. What clean-room or equivalent process lets me replicate functionality without copying code, interface, or branding?
3. Trademark, trade dress, and copyright risk. What should I register first?
4. Non-solicitation, confidentiality, and trade secret protections.
5. Review of open source licenses (MIT, Apache, GPL, AGPL) before use.

### G. Outsourcing company and AI tools

1. Master services agreement and statements of work, acceptance criteria, payment terms.
2. Is a written IP assignment to my US entity enforceable with a Vietnam-based contractor? What extra steps are needed?
3. Mutual NDA, restrictions on access to production data, security requirements, and subcontracting.
4. Clauses on AI coding tools and third-party libraries, including ownership of AI-generated code.
5. Tax paperwork when paying a foreign contractor (for example, Form W-8BEN) and cross-border data transfer.

### H. Cash and technician pay features

1. The software calculates commissions, tips, and cash owed to technicians. What disclaimers do I need so it is not treated as payroll or tax advice?
2. Legal risk in recording cash and tips (worker classification, record keeping).

### I. Operations and insurance

1. Procedures for complaints, incident response, refunds, and payment disputes.
2. Contract terms for an outsourced hotline (confidentiality, data access).
3. Which insurance to buy before onboarding customers: tech errors and omissions, cyber liability, and general liability.
